<p>The first draft is a low-fidelity sketch of the problem space. It shows you the shape of your ideas, the connections between them, and the places where your thinking is still fuzzy. It is a map of your own understanding.</p>
<p>To treat a first draft as data is to shift your mindset from one of judgment to one of curiosity. Instead of asking &quot;Is this good?&quot; you ask &quot;What does this tell me?&quot; You are not trying to produce a finished product. You are trying to generate information.</p>
<p><strong>What this changes in practice:</strong> Write your first draft as quickly as possible. Don&#39;t worry about quality. The goal is to get the ideas out of your head and onto the page, where you can see them.</p>
