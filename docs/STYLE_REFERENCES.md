# Style references: the weird register

Public work to look at before deciding how strange PMP's drawings should get.
Nothing here is to be copied; the point is to name what we like, then draw our
own. Links checked 2026-09-26.

For each one, note: **keep** (what we'd borrow), **avoid** (what we wouldn't),
and a score for how weird it is, from 1 (polite) to 5 (unsettling).

## A. Deadpan literary: ordinary scenes, one wrong detail, told straight

| Who | Where | Why look |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Tom Gauld | https://www.tomgauld.com/ | Tiny figures, flat colour, bone-dry jokes about big ideas. Closest to PMP's ink-and-paper style. |
| Edward Gorey | https://www.goreyhouse.org/ | Victorian deadpan, quietly sinister. The "nothing is odd here" tone. |
| Saul Steinberg | https://saulsteinbergfoundation.org/ | A single line doing conceptual jokes: drawings about drawing, maps that argue. |

## B. Gentle uncanny: creatures and objects with too much personality

| Who | Where | Why look |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Shaun Tan | https://www.shauntan.net/ | Strange creatures in ordinary places; melancholy, curious, never cute. |
| Poorly Drawn Lines (Reza Farazmand) | https://poorlydrawnlines.com/ | Deadpan animals and objects having existential moments. Simple drawing, weird premise. |
| Strange Planet (Nathan W. Pyle) | https://www.instagram.com/nathanwpylestrangeplanet/ | Everyday life described with alien literalness. A model for naming AI things plainly. |
| Gemma Correll | https://www.gemmacorrell.com/ | Quirky doodle personality, small absurd details. |

## C. Absurd machines: systems drawn as contraptions

| Who | Where | Why look |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Rube Goldberg | https://www.rubegoldberg.org/ | Overbuilt chains of cause and effect. An AI pipeline, drawn honestly, looks like this. |
| W. Heath Robinson | https://www.heathrobinsonmuseum.org/ | British cousin of the above: calm operators running ridiculous apparatus. |

## D. Hand-drawn technical explainers: tech made visual, and still accurate

| Who | Where | Why look |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Julia Evans, Wizard Zines | https://wizardzines.com/ | Hand-drawn zines that explain real systems correctly. The honesty bar PMP already holds. |
| Maggie Appleton | https://maggieappleton.com/ | Illustrated essays on tech ideas; metaphors drawn with care. |
| xkcd | https://xkcd.com/ | Stick figures carrying precise, often technical jokes. |
| Wait But Why | https://waitbutwhy.com/ | Crude drawings plus long-form explanation; proof that rough can teach. |
| Anatomy of an AI System | https://anatomyof.ai/ | An intricate systems map of one AI product. Reference for the reference sheets, not the jokes. |

## E. Bold graphic wit: one idea, one image

| Who | Where | Why look |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Christoph Niemann | https://www.christophniemann.com/ | Visual puns that turn a concept on its head in a single frame. |
| Jean Jullien | https://www.jeanjullien.com/ | Thick brush line, instant readability, gentle absurdity. |
| Nicolas Ménard | https://www.nicolasmenard.com/ | Odd, charming animation. Reference for how the drawings might move. |

## F. Composition and deadpan staging

| Who | Where | Why look |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Accidentally Wes Anderson | https://accidentallywesanderson.com/ | Symmetry, pastel, and staged stillness in real places. A reference for framing, not drawing. |

## How to use this

1. Browse, and pick **two or three groups** (for example A + C + D).
2. In each, mark one or two **keeps** and one **avoid**.
3. Say how weird overall, 1 to 5. (The current emblems are about a 2.)

From that we write a one-page style brief for docs/STORYBOARD_AUTHORING.md,
redraw three emblems as samples, and adjust before rolling it out.
