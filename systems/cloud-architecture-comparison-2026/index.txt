<p><strong>Key takeaways</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Cloud choice is now a choice of <span class="highlight">architectural intent</span>, not a service checklist.</li>
<li>Your authority hierarchy determines governance friction and operating risk.</li>

<p>As of February 2026, useful cloud decisions start from structure: how each platform encodes governance, data, intelligence, execution, and cost. This guide uses a five-layer model so you can choose based on how your team actually builds, not on whichever feature page you read last.</p>
<h3 id="how-should-teams-compare-aws-azure-and-gcp-in-2026">How should teams compare AWS, Azure, and GCP in 2026?</h3>
<p>Teams should compare AWS, Azure, and GCP by architectural intent rather than service-count marketing. This guide is for builders and operators choosing a cloud home for AI-era systems, and the practical question is which platform makes governance, state, intelligence, orchestration, and cost easiest for your real operating model.</p>
<aside class="callout callout-insight">
  <p><strong>Design note.</strong> Read this page as a tradeoff map: make boundaries explicit, track feedback loops, and optimize only after constraints are clear.</p>
</aside>

<h2 id="act-i-the-frame">Act I: The frame</h2>
<h3 id="the-five-layer-model">The five-layer model</h3>
<p>Use this stack from bottom to top:</p>
<ul>
<li>Authority hierarchy: identity, policy, and billing boundaries.</li>
<li>State layer: data models, consistency, and replication.</li>
<li>Intelligence layer: model access, evaluation, and guardrails.</li>
<li>Orchestration layer: serverless runtime, workflows, eventing.</li>
<li>Experience layer: product surface and delivery velocity.</li>
</ul>
<p>Two supporting layers cross all five: networking and cost architecture.</p>
<p>This is the core move in this doc: compare platforms by design constraints first, then by services.</p>

<p>Your hierarchy determines how hard governance becomes at scale.</p>

<p>AWS treats the account as the core boundary and uses SCPs for org-level guardrails (<a href="https://docs.aws.amazon.com/accounts/latest/reference/manage-acct-creating.html">AWS account boundary</a>, <a href="https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_manage_policies_scps.html">SCPs</a>).</p>

<p>GCP uses a tree model (Organization, Folders, Projects) with inherited access semantics (<a href="https://cloud.google.com/resource-manager/docs/cloud-platform-resource-hierarchy">GCP resource hierarchy</a>).</p>

<h3 id="layer-1-experience">Layer 1: Experience</h3>
<p>The experience layer should be chosen last, not first. If hierarchy, state, and orchestration are misfit, front-end velocity becomes rework.</p>
<ul>
<li>Use Azure-first experience paths when identity and compliance are tightly coupled to Microsoft tenancy.</li>
<li>Use GCP-first paths when data products and AI loops are the product core.</li>
<li>Use AWS-first paths when modularity and ecosystem breadth are your advantage.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="supporting-layer-networking">Supporting layer: Networking</h3>
<p>Networking architecture often decides whether multi-region growth stays simple or becomes fragile.</p>
<ul>
<li>GCP VPC is global, with regional subnets.</li>
<li>AWS VPC is region-scoped.</li>
<li>Azure VNet is region-scoped, with global peering options.</li>
</ul>
<p>References: <a href="https://cloud.google.com/vpc/docs/vpc">GCP VPC overview</a>, <a href="https://docs.aws.amazon.com/vpc/latest/userguide/what-is-amazon-vpc.html">AWS VPC user guide</a>, <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/virtual-network/virtual-network-vnet-peering-faq">Azure VNet peering FAQ</a>.</p>

<h2 id="act-iii-choosing-by-intent">Act III: Choosing by intent</h2>
<h3 id="selection-heuristics">Selection heuristics</h3>
<p>Use your dominant constraint to choose:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Governance-first intent:</strong> Azure often reduces policy drift sooner.</li>
<li><strong>Data/AI-first intent:</strong> GCP often reduces distance between data and model workflows.</li>
<li><strong>Modularity-first intent:</strong> AWS usually provides the widest composition space.</li>
</ul>
<p>For a student onboarding platform: if identity and compliance stack are Microsoft-centered, Azure is often the fastest institutional fit; if multimodal data and model workflows are core product differentiators, GCP is often the cleaner data-and-AI fit; if scale plus multi-model optionality dominates, AWS can be the strongest builder path.</p>

<h3 id="what-this-changes-in-practice">What this changes in practice</h3>
<ol>
<li>Start architecture reviews with hierarchy and state, not UI and SDKs.</li>
<li>Pick a cloud by organizational decision pattern, not by feature excitement.</li>
<li>Treat AI platform selection as a governance decision.</li>
<li>Make networking and commitment discounts explicit in design docs.</li>
