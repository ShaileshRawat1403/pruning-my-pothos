<h2 id="why-not-just-use-letters-or-just-use-words">Why not just use letters, or just use words</h2>
<p>You could give every character a number. Then the vocabulary is tiny, but every sentence becomes a long sequence, and long sequences cost more to process.</p>
<p>You could give every word a number instead. Then sequences are short, but the vocabulary has to be enormous, and it still fails the first time someone types a word that was not in it.</p>
<p>Tokenizers sit between the two. Common words get their own token. Rarer words get split into pieces that are themselves common. The vocabulary stays a manageable size, sequences stay reasonably short, and nothing is unrepresentable, because worst case a word falls back to smaller and smaller fragments.</p>
<h2 id="what-a-token-is">What a token is</h2>
<p>A token is one entry in that vocabulary. It might be a whole word, part of a word, a punctuation mark, or a space attached to the word that follows it.</p>
<p>So a made-up word like &quot;Smartification&quot; might arrive as three tokens rather than one, something like <code>Smart</code>, <code>ifi</code>, <code>cation</code>. The model sees three integers in sequence, not a word it recognises.</p>
<p>This is why one visible word does not reliably equal one token, and why character count is a poor predictor of token count. A short sentence of unusual words can cost more tokens than a longer sentence of ordinary ones.</p>
<h2 id="a-toy-version">A toy version</h2>
<p>Real tokenizers are built by algorithms such as byte pair encoding, which start from characters and repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent pair until the vocabulary reaches a target size. The merge table is learned from data.</p>
<p>The snippet below is not that. It splits on a fixed rule and learns nothing. It is here only to make the splitting step concrete:</p>
<pre><code class="language-python">import re

text = &quot;Smartification!&quot;
tokens = re.findall(r&quot;[A-Za-z]+|[^A-Za-z\s]&quot;, text)
print(tokens)
# [&#39;Smartification&#39;, &#39;!&#39;]
</code></pre>
<p>Notice that it keeps &quot;Smartification&quot; whole, which a real subword tokenizer would not. That gap is the point. A toy splitter shows you that splitting happens; it does not tell you how any particular model will split your text. For that, use the tokenizer that ships with the model you are actually calling.</p>
<h2 id="where-token-counts-show-up-in-practice">Where token counts show up in practice</h2>
<p>Two places, both operational.</p>
<p><strong>Context limits.</strong> Models accept a bounded number of tokens per request. The bound is counted in tokens, not characters or words, so the only reliable way to know whether something fits is to tokenize it.</p>
<p><strong>Cost and latency.</strong> Where a provider charges by volume, it charges per token, and processing time generally grows with sequence length. Estimating either from character count will drift, in a direction that depends on what you are sending.</p>
<p>Both of these are reasons to measure rather than estimate. Exact limits and prices change by model and provider, so check the ones you are using rather than trusting a number in an article.</p>
<h2 id="a-caution-about-what-tokenization-explains">A caution about what tokenization explains</h2>
<p>Tokenization is often used to explain why models miscount letters in a word, or stumble over comparing numbers. The intuition is reasonable: if a word arrives as one integer, the individual letters are not separately present in the input.</p>
<p>Treat that as a plausible contributing factor rather than a settled cause. This page has not measured it, and the failures people point to usually have more than one thing going on. The safe statement is the narrow one: tokenization changes how input is segmented and represented before the model sees it. Where it goes beyond that, say less, or go and measure it.</p>
<p>For what happens after the integers reach the model, see <a href="/systems/what-an-ai-model-actually-is/">what an AI model actually is</a>. For how the token budget for a single run is filled, see <a href="/systems/context-windows-as-working-memory/">context windows as working memory</a>.</p>
