<p>The model reads the text as <a href="/systems/a-simple-tokenizer/">tokens</a> and produces numbers for each one. One sentence needs one list, so the per-token numbers are combined. <strong>Sentence-BERT produces one embedding per sentence by pooling the model&#39;s per-token outputs, and its default is the mean.</strong> That averaged list is what people mean by a mean embedding.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-dimensions-are">What the dimensions are</h2>
<p>Each number in the list is one dimension. <strong>OpenAI&#39;s text-embedding-3-small returns 1536 numbers by default and text-embedding-3-large returns 3072, and a dimensions parameter shortens the list.</strong> More dimensions can hold finer distinctions; fewer are cheaper to store and faster to compare. The right length is the shortest one that still finds the right material on your own data.</p>
<p>The individual numbers have no names. Dimension 412 does not mean &quot;animal&quot; or &quot;formal&quot;. Meaning lives in where the whole list points, relative to other lists, which is why people call the collection an embedding space.</p>
<h2 id="how-similarity-is-measured">How similarity is measured</h2>
<p>Two embeddings are compared by the angle between them, called cosine similarity. Pointing the same way scores 1; unrelated scores near 0. <strong>OpenAI&#39;s embeddings are normalised to length 1, so cosine similarity can be computed as a dot product and gives the same ranking as Euclidean distance.</strong> For these embeddings, the two common distance measures put results in the same order.</p>
<p>A similarity score is a measure of closeness. It is not a probability, and 0.82 does not mean 82% sure.</p>
<h2 id="what-embeddings-are-used-for">What embeddings are used for</h2>
<p><strong>OpenAI lists search, clustering, recommendations, anomaly detection, diversity measurement and classification as uses.</strong> The best-known one is the retrieval step of <a href="/systems/retrieval-augmented-generation-in-plain-terms/">retrieval-augmented generation</a>: embed the question, find the nearest passages, and give those to the model. The same comparison groups support tickets by topic, finds near-duplicates, and flags the document that looks like nothing else.</p>
<h2 id="what-nearness-does-and-does-not-tell-you">What nearness does and does not tell you</h2>
<p><strong>Nearness in an embedding space measures what the model learned to treat as related.</strong> That is often what you want, and sometimes exactly what goes wrong.</p>
